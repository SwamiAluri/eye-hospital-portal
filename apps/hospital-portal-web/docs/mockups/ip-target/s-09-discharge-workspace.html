The last gate before the bed is released, shown in full — shell plus all five steps. Its single most important property is which money blocks discharge and which does not: the patient’s own obligation gates, the payer’s pending settlement never does.

Rule preserved from the ownership SoT. Discharge is gated on patient obligation only. A TPA settlement that has not yet arrived must never hold a bed, and the screen says so in words rather than leaving staff to infer it from a green tick.

Pharmacy charges post to the same bill. Anything dispensed at discharge must be on the bill before finalisation, or it becomes leakage in exactly the same way an uncharged implant does.

The prescription is part of the single post_op_instructions record (POST /api/ip-management/journeys/{id}/post-op-instructions), and the drug names come from the ophth_medication master via GET /api/ip-management/master/medications. The charge column is a read from Auth — adding a medication here does not create a charge line, which is why the completeness warning above exists.

A real path that must exist in the product, not an edge case to be forced through an override.

  • 3Blockers are grouped by category — clinical, patient obligation, coverage, administrative — so the nurse can see instantly whether the hold is hers to clear or the cashier’s.
  • 1The financial blocker links straight to S-03, so the hold can be cleared without leaving the workflow.
  • 1The coverage row is present but explicitly labelled non-blocking. Silence here is what causes wards to hold beds unnecessarily.
